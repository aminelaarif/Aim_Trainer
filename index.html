Free online aim trainer for competitive FPS players

Aim Range is a free aim trainer that runs in your browser. No download, no account: open the page and start aim training for Valorant, Counter-Strike 2, Apex Legends, Overwatch 2 and any other shooter. Each mode isolates one aiming skill, so you can see what is holding you back and practise it on purpose.

Aim training modes

  • Gridshot – speed and target switching. Three targets on screen at once; clear them as fast as you can without spraying clicks.
  • Flick – flick shots. One target at a time, always far from your last click. Snap to it in one motion.
  • Precision – micro-adjustment. Targets shrink until they vanish, so you have to stop cleanly and click.
  • Tracking – smooth tracking. Keep your crosshair on a target that keeps changing direction.
  • Reaction – a reaction time test. Wait for the target, then click the instant it appears.

Built for competitive aim practice

Play 30 or 60 second rounds with small, medium or large targets and your choice of crosshair. Personal bests are tracked separately for every mode, target size and round length, so your scores stay comparable. The results screen shows your accuracy and whether you overshoot or stop short on flicks. A five-minute warm-up runs Reaction, Flick, Gridshot, Tracking and Precision in order before you queue for ranked.

Sensitivity calculator: cm/360 and eDPI

The Learn tab includes a sensitivity converter for Valorant, CS2, Apex Legends and Overwatch 2. Enter your mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity to see your cm/360 and eDPI, plus tips on posture, grip, flicking, tracking and how to balance speed against accuracy.

Frequently asked questions

Is Aim Range free?
Yes. It is completely free, runs in your browser and needs no download or account. Your scores are saved on your device.
Does an online aim trainer help in Valorant or CS2?
Yes. Aim is built from separate skills such as flicking, tracking, micro-adjustment and reaction time, and practising them on their own makes them easier to use in game. Aim Range uses your normal desktop cursor, so it trains hand–eye coordination, clicking and target reading rather than your exact in-game sensitivity.
How long should I practise aim each day?
Around fifteen focused minutes a day works better than long sessions once a week. Start with the five-minute warm-up and stop when your hand gets tired.
What accuracy should I aim for?
Aim for 85–90% accuracy. Higher than that means you can go faster; lower means you should slow down.
How do I work out my cm/360?
Open the Learn tab and enter your game, mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity. The calculator shows how many centimetres of mouse movement a full 360° turn takes, plus your eDPI.
